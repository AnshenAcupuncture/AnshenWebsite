What we mean by “psychology informed Chinese medicine”

It's a phrase we use a lot, so it's worth explaining plainly. It isn't a separate technique — it's a way of paying attention.

When people read that Ānshén Acupuncture practises psychology informed Chinese medicine, a reasonable first thought is: what does that actually mean? It's a fair question, and the answer is refreshingly un-mysterious.

Chinese medicine already sees mind and body as one

This is the part that surprises people. Long before "mind-body connection" became a wellness phrase, classical Chinese medicine was built on it. The tradition never split the emotional from the physical. In its framework, the state of your mind and the state of your body are read as one continuous picture, two descriptions of the same person, not two separate departments.

There's even a word at the heart of our name for it. Shén means spirit, mind and vitality all at once. To care for the body without considering the mind, in this tradition, would be to only ever see half of someone.

Stress rarely stays in the mind. It shows up in sleep, in digestion, in tension, in the shape of a cycle. The body keeps a fairly honest record.

So what does “psychology informed” add?

If the tradition already holds this view, why say it out loud? Because naming it changes how the room feels and how the work is done. Being psychology informed means:

  • An assessment that genuinely asks about your stress, mood, sleep and energy, not as a formality, but as part of the clinical picture.
  • An understanding of how an overactive nervous system shows up in the body, and how a calmer one can support it.
  • A space that is unhurried and free of judgement, because feeling safe is not a soft extra; it's part of how the nervous system settles.

In practice, it means a session isn't only about where something hurts. We're just as interested in how you've been carrying it.

What this looks like in a session

Mostly, it looks like being listened to properly. There's time to talk before any needles are placed. Nothing is rushed, and nothing you mention is treated as off-topic or too small. From there, treatment is built around the whole picture: the physical concern and the person around it.

It's a quiet way of working, but it's a deliberate one. It's the reason the clinic is the way it is.

You can read more about how and why we practise this way on our about page, or come and experience it for yourself with an initial acupuncture appointment.
